Academic Report on NPTEL Courses:

Course 1: Probability and Stochastics for Finance
Course Code: 111104089
Instructor: Prof. Vivek Borkar, IIT Bombay
Duration: 12 Weeks
Type: Core Mathematical Foundations for Finance
---

Objective:
To introduce core concepts in probability theory, stochastic processes, and their application in financial modeling—particularly for derivative pricing and risk assessment.
---
Academic Coverage:

1. Probability Theory Basics

Sample spaces, events, sigma-algebras

Probability measures and Kolmogorov’s axioms

Random variables: discrete, continuous, and mixed

Distribution functions and expectation

Variance, covariance, and moments

Inequalities: Markov, Chebyshev


2. Convergence and Limit Theorems

Convergence in probability and almost surely

Law of Large Numbers (LLN)

Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

Borel–Cantelli Lemmas


3. Conditional Expectation

Definitions and intuitive understanding

Properties of conditional expectations

Applications in financial risk modeling


4. Martingales

Definitions of martingales, submartingales, and supermartingales

Optional stopping theorem

Martingale convergence theorems

Examples from gambling, insurance, and finance


5. Brownian Motion

Construction and properties of standard Brownian motion

Markov and martingale properties

Path properties and scaling behavior

Filtrations and adapted processes


6. Stochastic Integration

Construction of Itô integral

Itô isometry

Quadratic variation of Brownian motion

Simple and predictable processes


7. Itô Calculus

Itô’s Lemma

Chain rule for stochastic processes

Applications in option pricing models

Comparison with deterministic calculus


8. Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs)

Solving SDEs using Itô calculus

Strong vs weak solutions

Examples: Geometric Brownian motion, Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process


9. Financial Applications

Derivation of Black–Scholes Partial Differential Equation (PDE)

Risk-neutral valuation

Hedging strategies

No-arbitrage principle

Connections with Martingale Representation Theorem
---




Course 2: Probability and Stochastic for Finance II
Course Code: 109104104
Instructor: Prof. R. Srivastava, IIT Kanpur
Duration: 4 Weeks (~20 Lectures)
Type: Applications in Quantitative Finance
---

Objective:
To build on the foundations of stochastic finance and apply them to real-world financial instruments like bonds, derivatives, portfolios, and market models.
---

Academic Coverage:

1. Interest Rate Theory

Zero-coupon bonds and yield curves

Discount functions and spot rates

Term structure modeling

Forward rate agreements

Short-rate models: Vasicek, CIR models (overview)


2. Portfolio Optimization & CAPM

Mean-variance optimization

Risk–return tradeoff

Capital Market Line and Security Market Line

Derivation and limitations of CAPM

Efficient frontier analysis

Lagrangian and KKT conditions in optimization


3. Derivatives and Pricing Models

Discrete-time models: Binomial trees

Risk-neutral probabilities and arbitrage-free pricing

Replication and hedge portfolios

Option pricing via binomial method

Introduction to American options


4. Change of Measure & Girsanov’s Theorem

Concept of equivalent martingale measure

Radon–Nikodym derivative

Girsanov’s theorem: Intuition and simple applications

Use in pricing under stochastic drift

Connection to risk-neutral valuation


5. Black–Scholes Framework

Black–Scholes assumptions and derivation of the formula

Greeks and sensitivity measures

Pricing of forward contracts

Use in equity, commodity, and currency derivatives

Comparison with discrete models

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Together, these two courses deliver a comprehensive academic foundation for modern quantitative finance. The first course lays down the essential probabilistic and stochastic calculus toolkit, while the second course applies these tools to real financial instruments and models, including portfolio optimization, interest rate modeling, and derivative pricing.

This suite is academically rigorous and aligns well with master's-level coursework in:

Financial Engineering
Quantitative Risk Management
Derivative Pricing Theory
Asset Management Strategy
